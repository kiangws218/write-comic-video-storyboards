# Dialogue coverage and image-grounded performance

This file owns D/P/G routing, ordered dialogue logic, timing and first-pass acting quality. Use its acting principles for all character shots; selective cards reduce planning overhead, not performance quality. Action admission and phase anchors remain owned by `core-authoring.md`.

Before the first character batch in a new task, or after this module changes, read [acting-calibration.md](acting-calibration.md) once. It pairs actual passages from the user's 学园 reference with intentionally thinned rewrites of those same passages; the developed side is not a new AI-authored or independently video-verified example. It supplies the requested quality reference without requiring access to the original DOCX. Study its acting organization, not its gestures as templates or new source evidence; do not reload it for every panel.

## Selective routing

- `D`: several bubbles/speakers, mixed dialogue/thought/narration, a 28+ character Japanese turn, three clauses, or a meaning-changing exchange.
- `P`: a supported expressive change, changing repeated words, or a task/listener/contact relation that needs staged development, including short or silent beats. A 21+ character delivery prompts a development review, not automatic gestures: an evidenced neutral hold or object insert may clear P with a concrete `risk_basis` and natural timing.
- `G`: unresolved hand/grip/contact/overlap/topology/vector/perspective or an added viewpoint.

`D` invokes a compact turn/coverage plan. `P` invokes one compact performance card. `G` first inspects the 720p proxy and escalates only unresolved decision-changing details to original resolution. Neutral/object/cropped/informational shots need no artificial flags.

Long delivery still needs a performance/timing entry with rendered evidence, even when P is cleared; an intentional hold can be that evidence. `State`, neutral expression, silence or waiting does not by itself clear P or forbid supported local acting. A cropped expressive action can need P: cropping limits the available anatomy, not the right to develop the visible task.

## Dialogue logic before camera count

Record every bubble/box in visual order while the image is open. Preserve owner, kind, meaning, order and information in natural Japanese; Chinese belongs only in the later subtitle deliverable.

First create ordered semantic turns. Consecutive bubbles may share a turn only for one continuous delivery, overlap, or an inseparable prompt/reaction. Question→answer, accusation→denial, interruption, reveal→reaction and attitude reversal remain distinct turns even when one speaker continues.

Then create ordered coverage groups. One group may contain adjacent turns when all are true:

- their final Japanese plus pauses fit the shot naturally;
- one camera setup can show the speaker/listener/object/environment handoff without a hidden cut;
- the later turn is short and does not need an independent reveal, joke, reversal or reaction landing;
- supported action and focus progression keep the shot visually alive.

Thus `解释→质问→否认` may be three shots or `解释 | 质问+短否认`; the semantic order never changes. Similar required coverage is redesigned rather than deleted.

Each group maps to numbered shots; normally retain this panel's complete coverage in one clip. The clip need not be exclusive to this panel: group complete neighboring units under core authoring's clip-boundary gate, including a question in one panel and its short answer/reaction in the next. Only a naturally over-limit panel unit uses core's recorded overflow split, preserving full turns and continued acting. Separate semantic turns or camera shots do not inherently require separate generated clips. Restore the source composition once, then use supported speaker/listener/relationship/object/environment/detail coverage. Before prose, assign every added shot a distinct visual function and compare `dominant subject | scale | viewpoint/axis | placement/overlap | visible information` with both the preceding planned shot and the next source-panel composition. At least two meaningful dimensions must change against each available neighbor; a new focal length, focus transfer or tighter crop alone is insufficient. Redesign or merge before writing if this cannot be achieved from visible evidence. Person-free object or environment inserts are allowed with off-screen dialogue when the item/space and sound direction are established. An uncited view may not invent the unseen reverse side of a room, hidden pose, new object or contact.

## Timing

- 1–20 meaningful Japanese characters: normally one shot.
- 21–41: review supported development or a purposeful source-grounded hold; add justified coverage when one setup cannot carry the delivery readably.
- 42+ in one numbered shot, or three complete clauses in one continuous turn: split across genuinely different shots.

Estimate before clip boundaries from final Japanese: roughly six meaningful characters/second, plus about 0.25s for `、`, 0.45s for `。！？`, 0.6s for `……/…`, and 0.35s for an in-shot speaker handoff. Use natural floors: interjection 1–2s, short sentence 2–4s, medium 4–6s, long explanation 6–9s, two long clauses 8–12s. Concurrent speech and acting use the longer interval.

Use the user's `说到【台词】时` approach when a specific spoken phrase usefully locates a performance development. In final prose, write `说到「exact substring of this shot's final Japanese dialogue」时`; otherwise use `开口前/转折后/重音处/句末`. The cue can locate the visible speaker's action or a visible listener's response, with preparation before it and continued task/pose after it. It synchronizes an admitted performance, not the voice treatment owned by `【台词与语气】`, and never admits an event solely because its words are spoken. Do not invent a new gesture for every quoted word. Chinese semantic paraphrases and unfilled dialogue placeholders are invalid in final Japanese storyboards; update exact cues when final dialogue changes.

## Scene-led, readable 2D acting before the card

Start with the admitted principal action or an already visible task/contact/posture. Ask what the character is doing now, what supported stimulus changes it, and how the response alters the ongoing behavior. An embrace must actually form when its necessary lead-in is admitted; a speaking character already walking continues the evidenced travel. Lips, hair or camera motion cannot replace these main actions. If there is no task, an evidenced attention shift, reaction or deliberate hold is sufficient; never invent a busywork prop or errand.

Choose one supported immediate intention for the beat, not a new backstory. Design readable participation at this framing: principal body/task action when required, or attention, graphic expression and rhythm for a quiet close-up. Facial detail serves that participation rather than an unrelated smile cycle. Dialogue meaning may organize admitted local acting, but does not authorize the physical event it mentions. Speech and body may differ when source attitude supports it: a polite reply with attention elsewhere, or restrained words with an existing tense grip. Do not automatically illustrate each spoken noun, intensify emotion or invent concealed motives.

Compose a full performance proposal inside the established source boundary before polishing its prose. Ask how this particular character participates: what they maintain, interrupt, press, avoid or wait for, and what the visible partner's behavior makes them do next. A dialogue explanation may organize the timing of a visually grounded inquiry or rebuff; it cannot introduce a physical event. Richness comes from useful interaction, distinct main poses, changing participation and layered timing, not from subdividing lip articulation into many named details. A neutral or quiet character can still be specific through held facing, an existing task or contrast with a partner; do not manufacture busyness.

Useful organization is `ongoing action/state → supported stimulus → selective response → changed or continuing task/relation`. Plan participation through the actual exchange, not one opening gesture followed by a long generic wait. This is a reasoning aid, not a sequence every shot must display or a gesture per clause. A response can interrupt an action, hesitate partway, fail to complete or resume it only when that behavior is supported/admitted; do not invent a failed grab, release or transfer to add personality.

### Acting guidance: scene patterns, not a gesture inventory

Use the supplied 学园 reference's organization: an existing situation gives a response its purpose, and that response changes or continues the interaction. The following are alternatives selected from source evidence, not steps to concatenate or poses to insert in every shot:

- **Unequal exchange:** an engaged speaker receives an indifferent, restrained or occupied response. Where that attitude is visible, the listener can keep the established task or turn attention aside; let the response affect the speaker's next supported behavior. Write the difference between the two people, not synchronized nods or a smile cycle for each.
- **Inquiry or concern:** attention arrives and supported local acting makes the inquiry readable; participation continues while awaiting and hearing the reply, through the established task, attention or an intentional pause as appropriate. Choose facing, distance and extent from this character's current posture and relation; a question does not automatically require a tilt, a lean, a fixed angle or a frozen listening pose.
- **Interruption or recognition:** an established task is interrupted, slowed or continued selectively as the character registers the stimulus and answers. When a stop is admitted, the main body may arrive before hair, cloth or a carried object settles. Select the useful response and pause, not a mandatory body-part-by-body-part reaction chain; being called does not by itself authorize stopping or turning away.
- **Changed engagement:** an evidenced rebuff, acceptance or hesitation changes how the character faces or participates in the exchange. A bounded turn aside, withdrawal within the existing posture, or reorientation can carry that change when admitted; carry its consequence as a continuing posture/task/relation into the next beat, not an obligatory recovery and stop. Walking away, breaking contact, reconciliation or a new emotional reversal still needs its own source support.

### Legibility, timing and supporting detail

In a head/shoulder or medium shot, prefer meaningful changes in head facing, head pitch/tilt and the visible shoulder/torso relationship to an arc carried only by eyelids or mouth corners. In wider shots, use the admitted whole-body action or silhouette; in an extreme facial crop, a clear graphic expression change can be sufficient. Describe the useful starting pose, direction, extent/speed and final facing or held state. Distinguish eye direction from head direction; a glance is not a head turn. Never invent shoulders, hands or a named target outside the established view. Core authoring admits bounded expressive pose development; it does not require a second drawn pose for every local head movement.

Use the visible body's participation as well as the head: an admitted upper-body lean can bring a character into an inquiry; an admitted torso withdrawal or weight return can make a response settle. When stance/support is established, a weight shift can lead or follow the visible shoulder/torso change. Specify the visible result and its arrival, not just the abstract phrase `重心变化`. If the lower body is cropped, describe the visible torso/shoulder response without inventing a foot placement, step, seat or hidden support. A lean or weight shift that breaks an embrace, changes a grip or creates a new contact is not merely acting polish.

Render the planned performance as a connected account of what the person does and how their visible attitude develops. Where the image and admitted beat support it, name that attitude in familiar Chinese—such as `困惑地`, `有些不耐烦`, or `神情放松`—and connect it to the main action, expression or response that makes it visible. Describe a supported change rather than adding an emotion label to every sentence. A neutral hold is valid; visible attitude does not authorize a stronger emotional reversal or hidden motive. Vocal emotion and delivery remain in `【台词与语气】`.

Use ordinary, editable verbs and complete action phrases. Organize the paragraph around the character's main behavior and its meaningful changes; include facial, hand and secondary details where they clarify that behavior. Prefer `困惑地歪头看向画面左侧，眉头越皱越紧` to an unconnected list of joint positions and tiny movements, when those actions are admitted in that shot. This is a wording example, not a new source or a required gesture. Retain direction, contact, extent, timing and endpoint when they affect execution; use technical anatomy or exact measurements only when they resolve a real ambiguity. Do not enforce a sentence limit or hide necessary action detail to make the prose shorter.

Choose developments from changes of attention, intention or interaction, not one gesture per stressed word. Short shots may contain rich, fine-grained layered performance like the 学园 reference; preserve its actual task, partner response, body participation and useful secondary motion. Plan concurrent layers together and serial actions in their actual order, then express their connection naturally instead of turning each layer into a separate instruction. Neither duration, verb count, number of body parts, a fixed head angle nor a head-motion quota defines quality. Movement extent must read at the intended framing while preserving the source anchor and contact; do not qualify every movement as `微微/极小/轻轻`. A purposeful hold, neutral delivery or fragile contact need not acquire a turn or tilt.

Choose a readable arrival, ongoing development or purposeful pause from this beat; neither settlement nor movement-to-hold contrast is an obligatory ending. Select onset, speed, hesitation and overlap, not a universal half-beat delay. One established task may continue while attention reacts. A blink or local facial response may make quiet listening readable in an appropriate crop; hair, cloth and breath may polish supported consequences, but none replaces an admitted embrace, hand entry, travel or other main change. Keep details when they contribute to this performance; do not mechanically append blink, finger squeeze, head recovery and hair rebound to every ending. An endpoint can carry the changed attitude or ongoing interaction rather than return everyone to a neutral pose. A neutral face need not change because the hand is acting.

### Relationships and continuity

In a shared shot, choose an attention hierarchy rather than freezing everyone except the speaker. Visible listeners may continue established tasks and respond at different times/intensities; the response can affect the next speaker's supported behavior. Preserve each person's distinct source-supported attitude, posture and contact. Do not make everyone nod, turn or react together by default, or require every background figure to perform. An absent listener remains absent; use a justified derived view if their reaction needs coverage.

Carry the task, facing, attention, contact and supported emotional residue rather than restarting the same facial arc at each cut. Resolve physical state gaps under [core authoring's cross-shot continuity gate](core-authoring.md#cross-shot-continuity-direct-cut-local-connection-or-action-bridge), including returns after listener/insert coverage; this module does not add a second transition-admission rule. Render only anatomy/targets established in the current view. Every independently generated clip positively restates its current opening under core authoring; restatement is not a dramatic reset or replay of an already completed lead-in. Character references establish identity/design, not automatic personality or hidden intent.

Choose coverage to show that performance, not to evade it. If an established shoulder/torso/hand or shared relation carries the beat, retain it in the source-matching view; a subsequent face/hair/ear insert needs a stronger dramatic job than being easy to ground or unlike the next panel. A silent listener may hold deliberately, but their sustained presence can be the point of the scene. Source-imposed close crops remain valid; do not invent unseen anatomy or widen a cited composition to satisfy this guidance. Apply the added-view contract when another supported view is needed.

## P-card

Create only for `P` shots and immediately render it:

```text
O=<self-contained main pose, ongoing task/contact and supported immediate intention>
B=<the enacted main pose/action change(s) responding to this beat, with useful order/overlap>
C=<how that response changes or continues the visible task/interaction while preserving required contact>
L=<current facing/pose/task/contact at the cut, including any ongoing action or useful residue>
```

Every item must be visible in the panel or admitted under core authoring's temporal action design, including a bounded necessary lead-in to a single established anchor; two separately drawn poses are not required. A named target must be established in the current shot; otherwise use the visible screen-relative direction. Render rather than copy labels:

- `O`: current orientation and supported task/contact, including an admitted preparatory state;
- `B`: the actual developments through the exchange, tied to exact Japanese or generic nodes when speech drives their timing; supporting facial detail does not replace a needed physical main change;
- `C`: their physical/social connection, not an obligatory extra hand gesture or delayed hair motion;
- `L`: the current orientation/contact/task at the cut, not an instruction to stop. Continuing movement or an engaged pose is valid; camera landing belongs to camera design when moving.

Retain the existing `opening/beats/coupling/landing` fields, rendered `details` excerpts and serial/concurrent `intervals`; no new card or ledger version. `beats` records the actual supported developments, with no universal minimum or maximum count, including richly layered short beats. Longer expressive delivery normally needs more development or coverage, but word count alone cannot require another gesture. Evidence excerpts prove that planned acting was rendered, not that a quota was filled. All cards, evidence and review explanations stay in the internal ledger; only the executable performance is rendered in the formal six-field script.

Write this card as a plan, not as a wrapper assembled from the finished paragraph. `opening` describes only the state before the response; `beats` tracks participation through the actual delivery/listening phases (one continuous beat is valid); `coupling` explains what the response does to the ongoing task, partner or physical relation, not why it passes source admission. A lone performer can couple attention to an existing task; an intentional hold can couple unchanged participation to an ongoing exchange. State a persistent pose/contact clearly once where needed, then render developments or the useful duration of an intentional pause; do not pad the paragraph by repeatedly listing closed lips, fixed eyes, unmoving head and settling hair. Keep admission reasoning in `action_admissions/motion_decision`. `details` may copy final prose to prove realization, but does not replace the plan. Estimate actual overlap and any separately occurring entry, pause or tail; do not set both acting and speech to the full shot duration merely to fill the time. Each interval is sequential relative to other intervals; within it `concurrent: true` (also the legacy default) uses the longer duration, while `concurrent: false` adds speech and acting. Rich short-shot layers can genuinely be concurrent.

Repeated words become separate beats only when their intention or pressure changes. Differentiate the admitted participation, main pose, timing or intensity where useful; do not force a fresh head gesture on every repetition. Wind, particles, lip sync and camera drift enrich but cannot carry an omitted meaning-changing beat.

## Semantic quality gate

Render each detail only in its owning field: static composition/camera landing in `【镜头设计】`, subject/object motion in `【可见动作】`, and evidenced non-dialogue audio in `【声音设计】`. Judge equivalent wording by the enacted meaning, not the presence of `先/随后/迟半拍/最后` or a body-part vocabulary.

After core's source/admission and motion-realization checks, compare with the relevant calibration example's weak alternative on two axes:

- **Time-course:** what does this character visibly do through the actual speaking/listening phases? Can the main changes, overlap and continuing state be recovered, or is it one opening gesture followed by a long unchanged pose, lip sync and incidental hair? Locate any intentional pause and its useful duration/purpose. A whole-shot hold may be right, but "waiting" or "source-safe" alone does not explain its dramatic use. An endpoint is not a stop instruction.
- **Interaction:** how does the ongoing task, partner or supported stimulus shape each response and what follows? Would the same gesture paragraph fit an unrelated exchange? Check readability at this framing: head/torso participation when available, clear graphic expression or facial rhythm in a true close crop. Can an editor understand the supported visible attitude without decoding a list of body-part adjustments? Review wording and enacted behavior separately: readable wording cannot compensate for lost acting, and a populated card with only decorative motion fails an admitted moving beat. Local facial acting and quiet listening remain valid; action-count and compulsory body-motion rules are not the remedy.

Judge the whole exchange as well as isolated shots. If it still resembles the weak alternative, recompose the existing card from available pose/task/interaction rather than adding synonyms, a blink checklist or another cut. Preserve source attitude/contact and recheck affected boundaries under core's gate; preserve rich short acting when actual serial/concurrent timing fits. Deliberate silence/holds remain valid when their specific role is clear. Repeated "speak, recover, hold" endings need this review even when every card is populated.

Record the concrete outcome in the existing `revision_audit` or `final_audit.semantic_review`: identify affected targets, the visible temporal development/interaction retained or improved, or the specific purpose and duration of a retained hold, plus relevant framing/timing decisions and unresolved limitations. Reuse `advisory_dispositions` for emitted hints. A source-safe assertion, generic "author reviewed" or repeated admission explanation cannot resolve an acting-quality concern. This is a semantic authoring/review pass, not a second source ledger or a demand to reopen unchanged approved images. When a proposed fix affects anatomy, pose, framing or contact, follow core's change-scoped reinspection.

Unsupported facts, omitted admitted principal action, erased meaning-changing developments, an unintelligible temporal phrase, an unclear current state at the cut, or an unresolved acting-quality concern block delivery. Automatic acting hints schedule this review; they neither reject a valid hold nor certify a rich draft because of its wording. Resolve them from meaning and available evidence, not matching words, artificial cuts or making every character move. Report structural validation and acting-quality review separately; neither automatic checks nor a self-review promises the eventual video model's acting quality.
