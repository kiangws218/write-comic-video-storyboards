# Regression cases for skill maintenance

Read only when modifying or forward-testing this skill. These cases protect behavior; do not turn each case into a duplicated rule.

| Case | Required result |
|---|---|
| Panel shows left-hand skewer and right-hand map; dialogue mentions money | Keep eating and reading the map; do not replace them with coins. |
| Character wades through cave water behind rock columns | Treat as State; use water ripples and passing rock-column occlusion, not wall-bracing or crawling. |
| Close-up cough does not show the supporting hand | Do not add a hand bracing on a bench or wall. |
| Previous panel shows a medicine bottle; current face close-up does not | Keep the bottle off-frame unless an uncited shot reintroduces it. |
| Listener is absent from the cited speaker composition | Use an uncited reverse shot or hard-cut sub-shot; do not pull the listener into the cited frame. |
| Character looks upward in a high-camera view | Determine angle from perspective; do not label it low-angle because of gaze. |
| Subject is left-third or side-view | Preserve placement and viewing direction as separate facts; do not default to centered/front. |
| A two-person cited crop has one large foreground subject on the left, a partly occluded subject behind on the right, and a prop extending rightward | Trigger the compact composition lock; preserve screen-left/right, depth, overlap, and prop direction. Never rearrange subjects by speaking order, narrative importance, or inferred world-space direction. |
| A floor-level crop looks through a standing character's legs while two large object halves fill both foreground sides | Trigger the unusual-viewpoint composition lock; preserve the between-leg camera, near/far hierarchy, and visible object placement instead of rebuilding an ordinary distant full shot. |
| A close-up shows one hand gripping one cord connected to one oval moving end against a white background | Trigger the topology lock; keep the count, connections, gripping hand, moving end, and white background. Do not infer a second hand, repeated beads, or physical scenery from context. |
| A cited comic composition occurs midway or at the endpoint | Reference that phase directly; do not force it to be the opening frame or a static hold. |
| Adjacent panels explicitly show compression followed by launch | The phases may be connected in one continuous shot with both references in order; do not add circling, landing, or another attack. |
| Two adjacent numbered shots repeat one dialogue-panel reference, viewpoint, scale, and camera purpose | Merge them, or turn the later block into a genuinely different uncited reverse/insert. A new speaker alone is not a new shot. |
| A clip ends with an uncited reaction using the same character, side close-up, dominant cup, and dramatic function as the next clip's first comic panel | Compare across the clip boundary. Remove or merge the reaction, or change at least two meaningful dimensions such as subject focus plus scale or viewpoint plus visible information. |
| Timed prompt contains “保持原格姿势”, “按照原格处理”, or “不出现湖岸实景” | Fail structural validation; rewrite as positive visible content. Keep ambience, Foley, breath, silence, and music on a separate `声音设计：` line rather than mixing them into visible facts. |
| A `分镜` or `战斗段` describes only subjects, dialogue, and camera, with no visible backing | Fail the per-block background gate; reopen the panel and add the physical setting, graphic field, or frame-filling surface in direct positive language. |
| Prompt says `无实体背景`, `无独立背景`, `原格没有实景`, `盒外环境不可见`, or `不补画树林` | Fail structural validation; keep that classification internal and replace it with the visible color, gradient, speed field, texture, or close surface. |
| One speaker has 28+ meaningful Japanese characters in one continuous turn | Trigger visual-load planning by character count before timing. Preserve the source composition, then add source-compatible coverage when one view lacks meaningful change; at 42+ characters or three clauses, normally use at least two numbered shots. |
| Source bubbles are Chinese or another non-Japanese language | Translate every spoken line, inner monologue, and narration line into natural Japanese in the storyboard while preserving meaning, ownership, order, and information; reserve Chinese dialogue for the subtitle deliverable. |
| One panel contains five bubbles from two speakers | Trigger the dialogue ledger before drafting, preserve bubble order and ownership, estimate speech before clip boundaries, and split coverage rather than compressing all turns into the remaining seconds. |
| A clerk's small opening bubble is followed by a guest apology and inner thought | Record three rows with clerk/dialogue, guest/dialogue, and guest/inner ownership; do not assign the opening line to the guest or omit the final thought. |
| A crowded panel ends with a small listener reaction after two bystander lines | Map the final reaction as its own bubble; a structurally valid storyboard that omits it fails dialogue-source audit. |
| Combat panel shows only contact | Do not auto-create anticipation, travel path, recovery, and landing unless the source supplies them. |
| Every shot in a known location | Describe all useful local background actually visible; do not impose a fixed detail quota or repeat unseen environment text. |
| A root creature opens its mouth and screams in one reaction panel | Use a short burst of roughly 2–4 seconds; end early instead of filling the clip with prolonged screaming or shaking. |
| An exertion face and `?!` accompany “why won’t it come out?” | Describe strain, confusion, or impatience; do not infer combined shock and rage from punctuation alone. |
| A clean gradient portrait has no established backlight | Keep the gradient; do not add rim light, glow, flare, or particles for generic polish. |
| A forest scene switches to a source-supported solid or speed-line panel | Preserve the abstract background; do not reinsert trees, shrubs, or terrain into that cited composition. |
| A supported pulling action shows hand, stance, direction, and endpoint | State those visible anchors precisely and keep the prop name/material/color stable; do not add unsupported mechanics. |
| A dialogue shot contains a gaze shift, nod, and small hand movement | Run them during speech; duration is pre-action + max(speech, concurrent performance) + post-action, not a sum of every gesture. |
| A 5-second refusal repeats the same phrase while a shared prop is pushed back | Preserve the separate refusal beats: positive opening contact, ordered hand/shoulder/head or face response, supported delayed hair/cloth motion, differentiated breath/sound, and a concrete performer/camera landing. A comma-separated action summary or raw keyword count is not equivalent. |
| An expressive panel visibly shows squeezed-shut tearful eyes and a wide-open mouth | Animate transitions available from those visible anchors, such as eyelid pressure, tear movement, mouth closure between phrases, chin or shoulder coupling. Do not replace them with invented gaze changes toward off-frame targets. |
| A clip formerly used `0-3s` and `4-8s` ranges | Output `分镜1（3秒）` and `分镜2（5秒）`; reset numbering per clip and validate the summed duration. |
| Three adjacent clips contain a 3-second prop retrieval, a 2-second offer, and a 6-second handoff in one scene | Merge them into one 11-second generated clip with three numbered shots. Different camera setups justify new shots, not separate clips; split only for a real narrative break or when the combined phrase exceeds the clip limit. |
| A panel uses strong foreshortening, overlapping limbs, impact debris, or an off-frame attacker | Review it at original resolution after the default 720 px pass; verify motion direction, airborne/grounded state, contact, and foreground/background hierarchy before writing. |
| A cited action panel is called a key pose but the prompt changes its camera angle, left/right placement, overlap, silhouette, or contact | Fail the source audit. Restore the cited composition at its declared phase; place any different view in an uncited internal cut. |
| A source-faithful action clip benefits from foot/hand microcuts, an environment pause, or a reaction insert | Allow the uncited coverage when it clarifies the existing beat and preserves every source anchor; reject it if it creates a new route, contact, event, or replaces the source-important view. Every actual cut receives the next shot number and its own integer duration, while the shots remain in one clip when continuity requires it. |
| A continuous attack contains anticipation, acceleration, a readable body unfold, contact, and recoil | Keep the complete action in one clip when it fits 15 seconds. State the rhythm contrast and in-between concentration; when the camera cuts, give each resulting shot a number and integer duration. |
| A monster has no supplied reference asset and its lore name is visually opaque | Use a stable visual label with scale, color, surface, body type, and defining anatomy at the first appearance of each clip; do not rely on the lore name alone or alternate labels. |
| A character looks toward a poster shown only in the previous panel, while the current crop contains no poster | Describe the visible gaze as forward, downward, or toward the relevant screen edge. Do not put the poster in the character's hand or name it as the current-frame target unless a new uncited view establishes it. |
| Adjacent panels show load→launch→airborne rise and the complete phrase fits within 15 seconds | Keep all phases in one generated clip with references in order. Do not end one clip after launch and restart the next in midair. |
| Adjacent panels show strike travel→contact→immediate recoil and the complete phrase fits within 15 seconds | Keep the physical cause and consequence in one generated clip; a camera cut starts the next numbered shot with its own integer duration, but not a new clip. |
| A high-speed body crosses frame | A narrow directional air-distortion or screen-wash effect may follow the leading edge and collapse after passage; it must not alter the cited pose or screen direction. |
| A weapon makes decisive contact | A brief high-contrast impact frame using the established source or locked effect palette, or a compact pressure ring, may occur exactly at contact, followed by proportional material/environment response; do not stack unrelated flare, smoke, particles, and distortion. |
| A blade rotates under an established light source | A brief highlight may travel along the reflective edge and fade as the angle changes; do not add a generic rim around the whole character. |
| A panel follows a known character carrying someone, but the current panel is small, occluded, or visually ambiguous | Do not assign either person's identity from plot continuity alone. Confirm the name from the current panel plus an explicit character mapping or unique visible trait; otherwise use descriptive role labels. |
| A storyboard needs one look statement usable by every shot | Start with `场景色彩基准` covering only global tone, style, rendering or texture, contrast or saturation, and lighting behavior. Do not list concrete objects or prescribe object-specific colors there. |
| A colored panel shows a blue ceramic cup against an ochre plaster wall | Preserve the visible blue ceramic and ochre plaster in the environment/shot prompt. Do not recolor either object to fit an invented global palette. |
| A monochrome page shows a recurring wicker box, grassland, wooden house, and a two-tone gradient background | Treat ink and screentone as value/material evidence. Add a concise `非人物上色锁定` that assigns stable colors and materials to the box, grassland, and house and names both gradient endpoints; repeat the relevant colors where visible. Do not call them black, white, or gray merely because the source is monochrome. |
| Monochrome character panels are accompanied by supplied color reference sheets | Take hair, skin, eye, and costume colors from those character references. Do not infer character colors from black fills, white paper, or screentone density. |
| A location or prop appears colored in one verified panel and monochrome in later panels | Carry the verified source color into later appearances when identity and material match; do not invent a second palette or transfer the color to a merely similar shape. |
| A recurring non-character prop crosses clip or batch boundaries | Keep its locked material and base color stable in every visible prompt; allow only a temporary, causally supported lighting or effect tint. |
| One generated clip contains a speaker view, a reverse shot, and a prop insert | Output three consecutively numbered shots with a positive integer duration for each; do not hide the reverse shot or insert as an untimed line inside the first shot. |
| A 9-second three-person medium shot contains a question, rebuttal, and joke response | Keep the exchange in one generated clip when continuity fits, but default to separate numbered speaker/reverse/reaction shots; one shared composition is acceptable only when a source-supported shared action makes every turn more readable. |
| One numbered shot says `画面切到手部特写`, `再切到反打`, or equivalent | Fail structural validation. Move every actual camera change into the next consecutively numbered shot with its own positive integer duration. |
| One 12-second dialogue clip contains a speaker view, an answer reverse, and a listener reaction | Keep one clip and output three numbered shots whose durations sum to no more than the clip limit; do not confuse clip continuity with camera continuity. |
| One continuous throw runs through load, rotation, release, flight, and impact on a stable action axis | Preserve the complete physical phrase in one generated clip. Retain a shot while the camera setup truly stays continuous, and number only real camera changes; do not fragment it merely to satisfy a dialogue-coverage heuristic. |
| A combat panel contains a large cropped black shape crossing buildings and adjacent geometry shows a sweeping tapered body part | Trigger the topology lock and identify the supported tail/wing/limb plus its vector; do not downgrade it to a generic shadow, wall, or impact mass when contour and debris direction establish the body part. |

| One shot contains several short quotes totalling 21+ meaningful Japanese characters | Require 2–3 distinct supported acting details in the finished shot; quote splitting, lip articulation, wind, or camera changes do not satisfy the density. |
| A long-take exception is claimed but the prose contains composition and dialogue only | Fail admission and replan real coverage; the ledger's claim cannot replace enacted acting. |
| A neutral character receives a long line | Preserve neutrality; count one clearly held expressive state, then use source-compatible coverage if further acting is unsupported. Do not invent a hidden wish to cry. |
| Speech takes 5 seconds, preceding recognition 1, concurrent facial development 3, and final hold 1 | Estimate sequential intervals as 1 + max(5,3) + 1 = 7 seconds, not 5 or 10. |
| A hand-only crop carries speech | Write visible wrist/finger/arm behavior at semantic beats; keep face off-frame and source contact intact. |
| One bubble continues over two shots | Keep one source bubble with ordered segments, verify exact joined Japanese text and both targets, and do not inflate source_bubble_count. |
| Japanese dialogue says `こっそり開けるなよ` and the acting prose says “嘱咐别偷看时” | Replace the Chinese paraphrase with the exact trigger: `说到「こっそり開けるなよ」时`; update it whenever the final Japanese line changes. |
| Japanese dialogue contains `暗石区の入口`, while acting prose says `提到暗石区入口时` | Fail validation. Quote a verbatim final-dialogue substring such as `说到「暗石区の入口」时`; generic `句首`, `重音处`, and `句末` timing remains valid. |
| A source-supported two-person fight forms one clear 12-second action chain with stable axis, no timed dialogue, and several useful camera changes | Allow one `战斗段1（12秒·自动分镜）` block. Give only the total duration, ordered rhythm/coverage beats, opening state, and ending state; let Seedance allocate internal cuts. |
| One clip contains both `分镜1（3秒）` and `战斗段1（12秒·自动分镜）` | Fail structural validation. Choose one timing mode for the clip. |
| A fight contains long dialogue, an ambiguous attacker, a fragile hand/weapon contact, or a source composition that must land on an exact cut | Reject combat auto-coverage and use individually timed numbered shots. |
| An auto-covered fight says only “the two exchange hundreds of blows with rapid cuts” | Fail the combat audit. Replace it with ordered attack/defense causality, target ownership, screen direction, material response, and a stable endpoint. |
| A decisive blade hit needs stronger impact | Integrate one dominant contact effect and proportional material response into that beat: brief impact frame or compact flash, directional pressure/debris, local light interaction when luminous, then visible recoil/displacement and decay. |
| Every strike in an exchange uses full-screen flash, large shockwave, heavy shake, smoke, sparks, and debris | Fail effect hierarchy. Grade light/medium/heavy/climax intensity, reserve the largest treatment for the source-supported peak, stagger effect layers, and keep silhouettes/contact readable. |
| A luminous energy trail crosses a character and nearby wall | Give it a concentrated core, controlled fringe, precise motion direction, brief local color spill or reflection on nearby surfaces, and a decay that restores the scene lighting. |
| Concrete, metal, glass, and water all produce the same generic glowing particles | Fail material response. Use angular chips/heavy dust, hard sparks/highlights, thin shards, or sheets/droplets/mist/rings respectively, with visible inertia, drag, gravity, and collision. |
| A large impact is meant to feel monumental | When supported by the source, stage near, midground, and delayed background responses with parallax and different peak times; do not rely on particle count or permanent camera shake. |
| An episode-wide ledger was completed first and the images were closed before cited shots were written | Fail the workflow. Reopen the exact panel and write each `source_locked` shot while that image is visible; the ledger cannot replace the image. |
| A panel ledger accurately lists subjects but the generated shot adds a plausible table, second hand, or background object | Fail the source-diff audit. Every visible noun and relation in a cited shot must map to current-panel evidence; delete unsupported additions. |
| A source-locked ledger row lacks `viewed_while_writing: true` or `source_audit: pass` | Fail version-2 ledger validation. These attestations are set only after the open-image comparison is actually performed. |
| A shot says only `背景。` or `森林背景。` | Fail structural validation. Require a concrete positive physical setting, graphic field, or frame-filling surface, and match its excerpt to the ledger. |
| Long dialogue uses the template form `人物：“……”（……）` without `说/问/回答` | Count all quoted Japanese dialogue anyway; do not let syntax bypass the 28/42-character gates. |
| A 42+ character turn remains in one target shot without a recorded exception | Fail validation. Split it across genuinely different shots or record and enact `long_take_reason` plus supported performance and timing. |
| Three bubbles from two speakers remain in one target shot | Require at least two targets unless a source-supported shared composition is justified with `shared_reason`. |
| Strict source locking makes an action shot inert | Preserve the anchors, then restore supported temporal contrast, performance, medium response, motivated camera behavior, material response, and endpoint; do not solve the problem by inventing events. |
| An uncited reverse or insert is useful but receives a formal citation to a nonmatching source composition | Remove the citation and mark it `uncited_coverage` with purpose and evidence basis, or restore the matching source composition. |
| A three-second dialogue reaction contains a pause, gaze shift, one-sided brow change, lip tension, finger-pressure change, breath, and delayed cloth motion | Keep the micro-actions when they form one causal performance chain. Do not reject them merely by counting verbs or force them into serial timing. |
| Several neighboring dialogue shots default to pointing, open-palm explanation, nodding, or folded arms | Trigger a performance-diversity warning. Reopen the panels and prefer source-supported task, prop, posture, gaze, breath, or finger behavior. |
| A character is called by name while walking | When supported, stage attention and emotion through latency: locomotion stops first, hair or clothing finishes its inertia, eyes and mouth register, gaze drops or returns, then fingers or grip settle. Do not replace the whole reaction with an immediate turn and point. |
| A scene requests a work, studio, or director-style target | State the requested names once in the global look baseline, then translate the target into executable character acting, lens, focus, light, atmosphere, material, and color behavior. Do not repeat the complete style stack in every shot. |
| A backlit window, mist, steam, dust, reflective material, or strong sunset supports atmospheric optics | Rim light, Tyndall beams, floating dust, bloom, and lens flare may be used selectively with a stated source and affected surfaces. Do not ban them generically or apply all of them to every shot. |
| One shot declares both a large aperture or shallow depth of field and full/deep focus | Trigger a cinematic-quality warning and clarify the focal plane, focus transition, or intended depth behavior. |
| A visual beat includes meaningful wind, room tone, footsteps, cloth, prop contact, breath, or an intentional drop to silence | Add a concise `声音设计：` line with perspective and synchronization. Do not encode a new visual event only in the sound line. |
| An ordinary numbered shot is ready for delivery | Require self-contained `场景环境`, `环境音`, `镜头设计`, `可见动作`, `台词与语气`, `光影布光`, and `声音设计` fields. Do not merge camera or lighting back into an unstructured paragraph. |
| One independently generated clip says `保持原有`, `上一格`, `上一镜`, `只放大上一格`, or `沿用前镜` | Fail structural validation. Restate the current pose, head/face orientation, contacts, environment, camera and light directly in the current block. |
| A clip shows only a black-haired woman and an armored traveler, but its action says her gaze falls on `对面的两名少女` inherited from an earlier clip | Fail the clip cold-start gate. Establish that counted group in the current shot or replace it with an executable direction such as `看向镜头` or `看向画面右侧外`. |
| A generatable field says `不新增飞鸟`, `不要改变构图`, `不使用背景`, or another negative control | Fail structural validation. Delete the unsupported idea and describe only the positive visible result; do not expose internal exclusions to the video model. |
| A character turns during dialogue | Distinguish gaze direction from face direction; stage eyes, chin/nose line, head, neck/shoulders and hair with readable latency, then name the final face orientation and gaze point. |
| A 4–6 second dialogue shot has one static expression and no meaningful development | Trigger a visible-density warning. Add at least two source-supported semantic stages or split to a real speaker/listener/prop/environment coverage shot; lip sync, camera drift and particles alone do not carry the duration. |

Run `python -m unittest scripts/test_validate_storyboard.py` for deterministic validator cases. Forward-test on raw panels and compare against the remaining visual cases. Structural validation passing is insufficient; record separate source-fidelity and cinematic-quality verdicts.
