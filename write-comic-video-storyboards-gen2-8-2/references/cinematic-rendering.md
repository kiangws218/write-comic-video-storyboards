# Cinematic camera, light, and finish

Read after grounding and performance planning when cinematic finish is requested. Use the director round to design presentation and action; core authoring alone owns source-node preservation, neighbor contrast and next-node arrival. Missing visual proof is a warning, not camera permission.

## Visual baseline

Resolve backing authority under `core-authoring.md` before polishing it. For omitted scenery, graphic emotion fields or background palette/effects, read `background-design.md`; mood grading does not replace a continuous environment. Its non-diegetic graphic effects are distinct from the physical atmosphere below.

Use supplied references first. Otherwise choose at most one work, one studio, and one director-style reference only when each clarifies a compatible target. Translate names into a few executable properties—2D acting density, line/background treatment, warm-cool contrast, depth, or light behavior—and state the stack once.

## Camera and rhythm

Decide the shot's visual purpose before selecting movement. Compare useful moving and fixed presentations from the actual beat rather than accepting the easiest source-safe setup. Push/pull can concentrate or release attention; pan/tilt/follow can trace an established task, path or height relation; an evidenced arc or rising/descending move can develop power, scale or a shared relation. Select a readable extent and rhythm, not a universal `轻推/微移`. Fixed framing remains useful for a held reaction, contrast, awkward pause or fragile contact. Do not impose either a fixed-camera default or an episode-wide movement quota, and do not add actor actions merely to justify moving the camera.

Choose mode, purpose, opening, coherent path and landing before rendering. The actual camera field is the maintained camera plan; use a brief camera_decision only for a complex trajectory or unresolved alternative, without copying the formal prose into another form. Source-node framing preserves its useful visual intent with recorded panel-size adaptation; it is not a comic first-frame asset. Supplied full character references support all angles. Design unseen surfaces and coherent space as production choices; missing source evidence only warns. Preserve readable action, contact and next-node arrival. A continuous arc/rise is one trajectory, not a cut at every angle. High-speed follow specifies relative camera/actor speed, depth layers, occluders, target distance and the outgoing state.

In a camera-only revision, edit `【镜头设计】` and its camera metadata/excerpts, not performance beats, dialogue, timing, light or sound. Record the camera landing separately from the performer's unchanged endpoint. At sequence review, repeated fixed framing prompts a purpose check, not an automatic failure; intentional stillness remains valid. A moving shot needs a coherent designed path and useful landing; lack of a drawn path alone does not reject it.

Choose terms only when they change the image:

- lens/scale and function: wide for depth, normal for natural relations, telephoto for compression/intimacy, macro/long lens for supported detail;
- camera height/direction, optical axis, horizon/eye line;
- subject placement, negative space, leading line/vanishing point, foreground occluder, layered depth;
- non-contradictory focal plane or supported focus transfer;
- one coherent camera trajectory—fixed, push/pull, pan/tilt, follow, supported arc/orbit, rise/descent, or motivated handheld—with useful extent/rhythm and readable opening/landing.

When the camera moves during character performance, state what initiates the move, what relation or feature it follows, and where it settles. `小幅横移/轻推` alone is incomplete; the landing must preserve the performer or prop endpoint rather than merely naming a direction.

Camera clarifies action, attention, space, relation, emotion, or result; it cannot substitute for performance. A cited composition remains recognizable at its declared phase. A separately cut viewpoint is justified coverage. A continuous admitted trajectory may change angle/elevation within one numbered shot; it does not trigger the added-shot contrast gate at each intermediate frame or become a hidden cut. Actual cuts still need separate numbers and durations.

Review camera rhythm across the whole local event. When its dramatic change is approach, acceleration, separation or release, test whether an evidenced push/pull/follow conveys that change better than leaving all visual pressure to off-screen Foley. Same-axis reframing can develop established image planes without inventing a new side of the space. Keep a fixed presentation when its particular contrast or observation serves the event, not merely because it is easiest to match to a still.

Keep formal camera prose executable: the useful framing, movement/path, extent or speed profile, final framing and any meaningful focus progression. Do not append `让关系读清楚/把关注集中于/确保完整可读/呈现源格关系` or repeat the same crop as a purported landing. Put purpose, source matching, safety and review explanations in `camera_decision`/the existing audit. A fixed shot normally needs no separate landing paragraph; moving shots need a concrete final frame, not `最终清楚呈现`. This is a meaning-based edit, not a ban on `保留/清楚/焦点`: actual crop limits, focus planes and important occlusion remain executable information.

Plan acting before an optional tightening of the view. Preserve visible shoulders, torso, existing gesture and partner response when they carry its readable change. Do not repeatedly select face, back-of-head, hair or ear inserts just because their geometry is easy or their subject differs from the next panel: a coverage difference is necessary but not a dramatic purpose. A supported detail insert can reveal pressure, hesitation or information; a source-imposed facial crop can carry clear facial acting. Choose from those actual functions rather than forcing wider shots or camera motion.

For continuous action use temporal contrast: dense small changes/steady camera for anticipation; wider pose spacing and directional follow for acceleration; concentrated in-betweens for grip/balance/mechanics; purposeful slow motion for a supported perception, anticipation, turn, apex, contact or aftermath; compact contact treatment; proportional response; readable continuing or settled result. Keep one intention and consequence together when duration allows.

### Slow motion as an acting and rhythm choice

Actively consider slow motion where it makes an important action, reaction or pressure readable, especially when the user asks for it. It is not restricted to combat contact and need not end at impact: a supported turn, delayed recognition, loss of balance or emotional response may carry the slowed interval. It changes the time course of an admitted event, not permission to invent another event. Avoid both a blanket slow-motion ban and automatic use in every shot.

In `【镜头设计】`, identify the entry beat, the interval being slowed and the return to normal speed or the deliberately slowed state at the cut. Use concrete phase wording; numerical speeds or timestamps are optional when they improve execution. State the coherent camera behavior separately: a push/follow can accompany slowed subject motion, while an unqualified fixed camera cannot simultaneously advance. In `【可见动作】`, enact what continues through that interval—weight transfer, turn, expression, contact response or existing task—rather than replacing it with a frozen pose and drifting hair. A purposeful held instant remains a distinct rhythm choice.

Budget the actual output duration of the slowed action in the existing timing intervals. Do not delete its dramatic landing merely to make a clip shorter; regroup or split at a supported boundary under core authoring. Slow motion does not automatically stretch dialogue or all sound: preserve natural speech and intentional lip synchronization, and specify any selected Foley/breath treatment in its owning field. Camera movement, subject speed and audio timing must describe one executable passage rather than competing instructions.

## Lighting and materials

Derive light from the environment and people positions. State source, direction, occlusion, main/fill or reflection, shadow boundary/depth, motivated rim/highlight, and local material response. Light may evolve with supported movement but cannot erase a face, hand, contact, or anchor.

Connect light to visible material: hair translucency; skin/eye catchlight; cloth folds/compression; diffuse wood grain; metal edge reflection; glass reflection/refraction; wet reflection; crystal transmission; steam/haze scattering. Direction and decay follow the source.

Atmosphere is conditional:

- 丁达尔光: directional light plus haze/steam/dust and an opening;
- 微尘/薄雾: plausible particulate or atmospheric source;
- 镜头炫光: bright source near/crossing the optical axis, brief;
- 轮廓光: side/back source following the silhouette;
- bloom/glow/overexposure: local peak followed by decay.

Use only effects with a job; do not stack them by habit.

A graphic star, flat stripe or soft-focus color patch in an admitted emotion field is not a physical light source, fog bank or magic event. It needs dramatic purpose and a coherent designed visual form, honestly distinguished from an inspected reference, not invented weather or automatic light spill onto the performer. Physical atmosphere still needs the visible cause above.

## Effects and finish gate

An effect has onset, peak, interaction, and decay beside its visible cause. Align speed treatment with the established vector. Use one dominant impact treatment and material-specific response: concrete chips/heavy dust, metal spark/highlight, glass shards, water sheet/droplets/mist/rings. Reserve the largest response for the supported peak.

Ambiguous ink flecks or graphic impact marks are not automatically physical blood, debris or particles. Resolve their role from inspected evidence; a graphic impact treatment needs no invented material source. Effects remain subordinate to the admitted event and its readable response.

Confirm source composition/facts remain recognizable; camera has one reason; focus/depth agree; light/atmosphere have a source; material response is local/directional; effects peak and decay; new shots meet core’s one-significant-difference rule against both actual final neighbors; and polish has not replaced acting, dialogue readability, or the endpoint.
