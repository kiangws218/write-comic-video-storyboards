# Cinematic quality without source drift

Use this module when a beat needs performance development, continuous action, cinematic coverage, or effects. Source locks and shot-role permissions in `source-grounding.md` remain authoritative.

## Quality principle

Grounding is not permission to produce static panel captions. Preserve every source anchor, then improve readability through supported performance, temporal contrast, camera restraint, material response, and carefully admitted coverage. A cinematic choice is useful only when it clarifies an existing action, space, relationship, emotion, or result.

## Continuous-action unit

Identify one uninterrupted intention and physical consequence before setting clip boundaries, for example:

- crouch/load → push-off → airborne rise;
- reach → grip → pull → object moves;
- wind-up → travel → contact → immediate recoil;
- slip → fall → contact → first settled pose;
- throw → flight → impact.

Keep the unit in one generated clip when its natural duration is within the model limit. Internal hard cuts may receive separate numbered shots, but do not hand an unfinished physical phrase to the next clip.

Split at a stable state, intentional hold, completed result, clear change of intention, time/location jump, or unavoidable duration limit. Do not combine merely adjacent actions with different intentions.

## Action admission

For a source-visible phase B, default to B. Add at most one preceding A, following C, or refinement of B when it materially improves continuity and passes all three tests:

1. **Evidence:** the current or adjacent panel supports it;
2. **Visibility:** it can be seen in the declared framing and occlusion;
3. **Anchor safety:** it preserves angle, placement, pose, hands/props, overlap, contact, and background hierarchy in a cited shot.

If a necessary phase changes composition, make it a separately numbered `source_supported_phase` or `uncited_coverage` shot. Do not invent off-frame bracing, hidden contact, grip changes, routes, rebounds, secondary attacks, follow-through, or recovery to make motion look complete.

## Rhythm inside a shot

Use temporal contrast rather than equal weighting:

- **Anticipation/emotional hold:** denser in-betweens, small readable changes, stable camera, brief supported hold.
- **Acceleration/rush:** wider pose spacing, fewer in-betweens at peak travel, directional follow or screen wash, delayed cloth/hair/debris response.
- **Readable mechanics:** concentrate in-betweens around grip, unfolding, balance correction, or other mechanically important phase.
- **Selective slow motion:** reserve for realization, near miss, apex, or pre-contact when spatial direction remains clear.
- **Impact:** compress contact to one or two graphic frames when useful, then show proportional recoil, deformation, debris, water, or camera settle.
- **Aftermath:** reduce motion long enough for the result pose or changed environment to register.

State only rhythm changes that materially affect generation. Attach quality terms such as speed, weight, pressure, or tension to visible staging, timing, light, or effects; do not append generic cinematic adjectives.

## Performance quality

Follow the character-count and coverage rules in `dialogue-coverage.md`. Performance must be visible, source-compatible, and readable at the declared scale.

Use eyes, face, head/body, and visible hand/prop relations across semantic beats. A held expressive state may count once. Preserve supported neutrality instead of inventing suppressed grief, tears, rage, or other hidden motives.

Run performance concurrently with speech when natural. Do not sum every gesture as serial time. For off-screen speech, use supported listener, hand, prop, or environment development. A long take is admitted only when its sustained action or relationship is enacted in the finished prose; a ledger reason alone is insufficient.

## Camera and coverage

Use hard cuts by default. Select one primary camera behavior per actual shot: fixed, restrained push/pull, pan, follow, handheld response, or another motivated movement. Camera motion cannot substitute for actor performance or physical action.

An added reverse, insert, environment pause, foot/hand microcut, or reaction earns its place only when it adds a distinct focus, scale, viewpoint, visible fact, or dramatic function. Keep the source-important composition as a recognizable phase; do not replace it with more attractive coverage.

Seven seconds is an audit trigger, not a mandatory cut. Retain a longer shot when supported action or performance genuinely develops; split it when the visual focus changes or the frame would otherwise remain static.

## Effects and material response

Place a motivated effect beside the action that causes it. An important effect has a readable onset, peak, interaction with nearby material/light, and decay. It strengthens speed, contact, damage, or scale without obscuring source silhouettes, contact, or spatial relations.

- High-speed motion may use narrow directional air distortion, screen wash, or a brief trail aligned with the established vector.
- Decisive contact may use one dominant impact treatment: a brief high-contrast frame, compact flash, pressure ring, or directional debris response.
- Reflective material may carry a traveling highlight only under an established light source.
- Luminous energy may cast brief local color spill and reflection, then decay back to the established scene lighting.
- Concrete produces angular chips and heavy dust; metal hard sparks and highlights; glass thin shards; water sheets, droplets, mist, and rings.

Grade intensity across an exchange. Reserve the largest flash, shockwave, deformation, or camera response for the source-supported peak. Do not stack flare, smoke, particles, glow, distortion, and permanent shake on every hit.

Rim light, lens flare, floating particles, volumetric beams, and generic glow require a visible source effect, established physical light, or specific story need. Effects may colorize a monochrome source function using locked production colors but may not deform a cited anchor beyond recognition.

## Quality-preservation audit

Before approval, verify that stricter grounding has not removed:

- the complete supported action phrase;
- readable semantic performance during dialogue;
- a real endpoint or result pose;
- temporal contrast between anticipation, acceleration, contact, and aftermath;
- material-specific response and local light interaction;
- useful coverage that clarifies space or emotion without replacing a source panel.

If a shot feels inert, first add supported timing, performance, medium response, or camera restraint. Do not solve inertness by inventing new events or changing the source composition.
